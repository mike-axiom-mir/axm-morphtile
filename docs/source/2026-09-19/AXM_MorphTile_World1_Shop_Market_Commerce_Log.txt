AXM / MORPHTILE WORLD #1 — DESIGN LOG
SHOP, MARKET & PLAYER COMMERCE SYSTEM
Mike — Axiom/Mir
Date: 2026-09-19

STATUS
World #1 design direction. These are game/world rules, NOT MorphTile defaults.

ROOT DESIGN PRINCIPLE
Keep the economy simple at the rule level but deep through interaction.
Trade should not collapse into one global menu where every transaction is functionally identical.
Convenience may cost more; direct human/player interaction should not be taxed merely for existing.

THREE TRADE LAYERS

1. AUCTION HOUSE — MAXIMUM CONVENIENCE
Purpose:
A global/high-reach option for players who value speed and convenience.

Rules:
- Easy listing and purchasing.
- Useful for common items, quick trades and broad reach.
- Intentionally high transaction cut.
- Cut applies to BOTH buyer and seller.
- The high cost is the price of convenience.
- It should not automatically become the economically optimal way to trade everything.

Design intent:
The auction house remains useful without replacing physical markets, discovery, negotiation or player-created commerce.

2. PHYSICAL PLAYER MARKETS — ZERO CUT
Purpose:
Direct, social commerce in actual world locations.

Rules:
- Players physically meet and trade.
- No transaction percentage cut.
- Players can negotiate, barter, sell items, offer crafting/services, take custom orders, etc.
- Resort areas, towns, events, clan spaces and naturally popular locations can become physical markets.

Design intent:
If players are spending their own time and location to meet and trade, the game does not take a percentage simply because the transaction happened.

3. POPUP STORES — PAY FOR THE SHOP, NOT THE SALE
Purpose:
Let players create temporary stores anywhere the world permits.

Rules:
- A player deploys a popup store at a chosen world location.
- The popup has a defined lifetime.
- Creating/deploying it costs a fixed amount of whatever resource/material/money is eventually chosen during balancing.
- NO percentage transaction cut after deployment.
- The owner stocks it from their own inventory.
- The owner sets prices/trade conditions within world rules.
- The popup remains in the world while the owner adventures elsewhere or is offline.
- Other players can discover it physically, inspect it and buy from it.
- Expiry, inventory and transactions are persistent deterministic world state.

CORE ECONOMIC IDENTITY
Auction house:
Pay heavily for convenience.

Physical/P2P:
Spend time/location; no transaction cut.

Popup:
Pay for temporary infrastructure; keep the proceeds.

PLAYER-CREATED MARKET GEOGRAPHY
Popup stores should allow market locations to emerge instead of AXM declaring every commercial district.

Example:
A dangerous cave consumes repair materials.
One player places a repair-material popup near its entrance.
It sells well.
Other players notice and place food, gear, crafting materials and services nearby.
The location organically becomes a market hub because players actually need it.

Likewise:
- tower entrances can become trade hubs;
- transport junctions can become trade hubs;
- newly unlocked regions can create temporary boom markets;
- remote specialist merchants can become destinations;
- competition can create clusters and price pressure.

Location becomes economically meaningful without requiring AXM to sell artificial virtual land.

POPUP MODES

A. BASIC UNATTENDED POPUP
- Fully deterministic.
- Fixed stock.
- Fixed prices/trade rules.
- Defined duration.
- No AI required.
- Works while owner is offline.

B. NPC-RUN POPUP
- Owner assigns an actual in-world NPC/companion to run the shop.
- NPC physically remains associated with the shop while the owner adventures or logs out.
- The NPC is a world actor, not merely a shop UI.
- NPC skills/capabilities may later affect what services the shop can provide.
- Recalling/removing the NPC removes that attendant function.

C. PLAYER-OWNED AI-DRIVEN NPC
Later capability:
The NPC can optionally be driven by the PLAYER'S OWN AI instance.

Possible AI sources:
- local model;
- user's chosen cloud AI;
- other compatible user-controlled AI bridge.

The AI is an optional brain for an existing world actor. It does not own the economy.

Possible bounded shopkeeper capabilities:
- discuss stock;
- explain items;
- advise customers;
- negotiate inside owner-defined limits;
- offer permitted bundles;
- accept declared barter conditions;
- remember allowed shop/customer context;
- provide crafting/gear knowledge when that NPC has the relevant capability.

The deterministic world remains authoritative for:
- item existence;
- inventory;
- ownership;
- prices/ranges permitted by owner;
- transaction settlement;
- currency/material transfer;
- preventing duplicate spending/items;
- shop expiry.

An AI may propose or negotiate a transaction; world rules validate and execute it.

AI SHOP INFRASTRUCTURE
A small service/infrastructure fee may later be appropriate for enabling an AI-driven NPC shop.

If the user supplies their own local/cloud inference, AXM should not pretend it is selling that inference.
Any AXM-side fee should correspond to actual service/infrastructure provided.

AI instances should not need to reason continuously.
A shopkeeper specialist can sleep when nobody is interacting and wake when needed.

SPECIALIST POSSIBILITY
An AI-backed NPC may eventually contain bounded specialist roles such as:
- merchant;
- social;
- crafting;
- equipment;
- appraisal;
- negotiation.

Only relevant specialists need to wake.

SHOP EVOLUTION / LEGO
The commerce primitive can gain richer physical forms as later MorphTile layers unlock more LEGO.

Possible progression:
- blanket + box;
- tent stall;
- vending machine;
- mobile caravan;
- companion merchant;
- automated workshop;
- custom player-built commercial machines.

The economic primitive remains understandable while presentation and capability expand.

OFFLINE PERSISTENCE
The popup is separate persistent world state from the owner's currently active character.

Owner offline does NOT imply shop disappears.

A deterministic popup can persist using:
- owner identity/reference;
- committed stock;
- pricing/trade rules;
- expiry;
- transaction receipts;
- resulting proceeds.

An NPC-driven popup additionally persists the assigned NPC's relevant world state.

AI is optional and must never be required for basic offline shop persistence.

PEER-TO-PEER DIRECTION
Explore whether popup shops can participate naturally in AXM's peer-to-peer multiplayer architecture.

Desired principle:
The shop should be representable as portable/verifiable state plus deterministic transaction rules rather than requiring a permanently simulated centralized merchant.

Do not weaken anti-double-spend/ownership authority merely to make this P2P.
Prototype and verify before claiming it works.

RELATION TO WORLD #1 ECONOMY
This market design connects with the other current World #1 ideas:
- materials are important for gear repair/crafting;
- crafting/repair skills can reduce material waste;
- vault contents are protected;
- worn pocket storage is protected;
- larger carried bags may lose a small bounded random portion on death;
- some carried money may be lost;
- preparation affects expedition risk;
- the resort acts as an early safe/home/service space.

Markets therefore support actual world needs rather than existing only as a money-making minigame.

AI TEST-GROUND VALUE
World #1 can eventually test AI in multiple bounded roles:
- player companion;
- independent machine-controlled player;
- shopkeeper NPC;
- crafting specialist;
- scout;
- other world actors.

This also creates useful future training/testing ground for WALMI while preserving the distinction between:
1. WALMI's own separate MorphTile world/save; and
2. WALMI's ordinary player profile inside Mike's adventure world.

ROOT BOUNDARY
These commerce rules belong to Mike's World #1.
MorphTile itself should provide neutral primitives such as persistent matter/state, ownership, capabilities, sleep/wake, interfaces, events, networking hooks and verification.
Other MorphTile worlds remain free to implement completely different economies.

SHORT PRINCIPLE
Convenience has a price.
People make markets.
Popup stores make geography matter.
AI may run an NPC; it does not own the transaction.
Player-driven. World-powered.
